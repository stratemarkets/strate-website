      Performance and memory#

      • The engine counts operations instead of time, so a script behaves the same on a fast and on a slow machine. If you reach the budget, look first for loops over large collections run on every event.
      • A collection operation (sum, sort, keys, percentile…) costs about 1 + n/8 operations.
      • mbo.large(min) costs about one operation per 64 resting orders.
      • mbo.level(…) totals are cheap; depletions, replenishments, refill_* and time_at_best scan the window once per call, on first read.
      • level_history: keeps every event of every price for its duration. On a very active market a long history uses a lot of memory: keep it as short as your windows allow.
      • Strategies and research scripts with a fixed order-by-order start read data as a stream: memory stays roughly constant whatever the length of the run.

      Not supported#

      • Real orders. Strategies are always simulated.
      • Several positions at once, pyramiding, direct reversal, trailing stops (trail: does not exist; manage a stop with exit() from your own logic).
      • Dates as values (2025-01-02) and ranges outside for.
      • Generic set<…>, heap<…>, deque<…>, series<…>; window<T>(…) (write window(d)).
      • Imports of more than one level, and import of anything other than strate/patterns/<file> and my/<name>.
      • Unicode escapes in strings (\u{41} stays as written).
      • persist in strategies and research scripts.
      • Book snapshots, footprints, request(…, symbol:) and drawings in long backtests.
      • request(…, symbol:) in the built-in check (it has no other instrument: E936).
      • Reading the future: negative history indexes, the footprint of a bar inside a handler, and any data after the Replay cursor.
      • Header keys and named arguments that are not documented are ignored, not rejected.
